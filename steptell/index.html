Steps Recorder replacement

Windows Steps Recorder is going away. Steptell takes over.

For years, Steps Recorder (psr.exe, also known as Problem Steps Recorder) was how IT asked people to show a problem: start recording, reproduce it, send the ZIP. Microsoft has deprecated it: it is no longer updated and will be removed in a future release of Windows. The tools suggested instead, such as Snipping Tool and Clipchamp, record video, not numbered steps.

Steptell works the way Steps Recorder did, with one hotkey to start, a step and a screenshot for every click, and a ZIP for the helpdesk. Then it lets you turn the result into a guide people can follow. It even opens your old Steps Recorder files, so nothing you recorded is lost.

Private by design

Your screen, your steps, your PC

Recording is always visible, typing is never captured, and nothing leaves your PC unless you export it.

Steptell never records your keystrokes. If you choose, steps can show short text you typed, such as a search term, but never passwords or anything that looks like personal information. Password fields are blurred automatically, and email addresses, phone numbers and card numbers are found in screenshots so you can blur them before sharing. Guides are encrypted on your PC. Steptell works fully offline and sends nothing anywhere.

  • Keystrokes are dropped the moment they happen. Only shortcuts such as Ctrl + S become steps.
  • Showing short typed text in a step is off by default, and never applies to password boxes or private browser windows.
  • Password boxes are blurred in every screenshot automatically.
  • Text recognition built into Windows finds emails, phone numbers, card numbers, IBANs and IP addresses so you can blur them before sharing.
  • Password managers and any other apps you choose are never captured.
  • Your guide library is encrypted with AES-256 on your PC, and exports can be password-protected too.
  • No account, no ads, no telemetry and no network connections of its own.
  • There is no hidden or background recording mode. A recording indicator is always on screen.
